How to Sell Digital Products on Your Own Domain: Pricing, Platforms, and Payments

The shape of selling on your own domain

Selling from your own domain does not mean you build a checkout from scratch. It means the page people land on, the address in the browser bar, and your email list all belong to you. The money part can live on a platform, on Stripe, on PayPal, or on a mix.

A typical flow looks like this:

Post or ad  →  your page (yourbrand.com/product)  →  checkout  →  automatic delivery  →  follow-up emails

Your page does the persuading. Checkout does the transaction. If you have not put a page online yet, begin with hosting a site free on GitHub and Cloudflare Pages.

How to price a digital product

Price changes what buyers believe about the product and decides whether advertising can pay for itself. These are rules of thumb, not laws. Test them on your own audience.

Price zoneWhat it usually means
Under $10Casual purchases. Paid traffic rarely works because the cost to win a customer can exceed the sale.
$17 to $37The impulse zone. High enough to signal value, low enough for a quick decision. A common sweet spot for a first paid guide.
$47 to $97Buyers want proof: testimonials, a detailed page, or an existing relationship with you.
$97 and upCourses and coaching. Trust and a clear outcome come first.

A five-step way to set the number

  1. Research five to ten similar products and note the range. Price for the value you deliver, not the lowest competitor.
  2. Do the ad math. If you sell at $27 and it costs $15 to win a customer, you net about $12 before payment fees. At $7 the same $15 loses money.
  3. Start slightly high and discount later. Launching at $27 with a limited $17 offer beats starting at $17 and raising the price. Only promote a discount that is real and has an actual end.
  4. Show a regular price next to the sale price only when the regular price is the one you will actually charge afterward.
  5. Decide your refund stance up front and say it plainly. A guarantee can lift conversion at higher prices. Instant digital files are a reason some sellers choose not to refund, but be clear either way.

Fit the price to the format, too. As rough ranges: single templates $7 to $17, ebooks and guides $7 to $47, structured workbooks $17 to $47, courses $97 and up.

Stan Store vs SamCart vs Gumroad vs Payhip

Each one bundles a storefront, checkout, and file delivery. What differs is how you pay and what you get. Figures below were checked against recent pricing roundups in October 2026. Prices change, so confirm on each company's own pricing page before you decide.

PlatformHow you payNotable
GumroadNo monthly fee. Roughly 10% + $0.50 on direct sales (30% on sales through its Discover marketplace).Acts as merchant of record and handles sales tax collection worldwide. Fees are not refunded when you refund.
PayhipFree plan at 5% per sale. About $29/month at 2%. About $99/month at 0%.Processor fees (Stripe or PayPal) apply on top at every level.
Stan StoreAbout $29/month, or about $99/month for the higher tier. No Stan percentage fee.Link-in-bio storefront built for creators. Processor fees apply.
SamCartAbout $79/month for Core, about $199/month for Pro (annual billing is discounted).Order bumps and one-click upsells are on Pro. PayPal is supported on all plans. Processing is typically 2.9% + 30¢.

Notice the two pricing shapes. Percentage platforms (Gumroad, Payhip free) cost nothing until you sell, then take a slice. Flat-fee platforms (Stan, SamCart) cost the same whether you sell nothing or a lot. Neither is better. They are better at different volumes.

A worked example. At $27, a 10% + $0.50 fee is $3.20 per sale. A $29/month flat fee costs less once you make roughly 10 sales a month. A $79/month plan needs more than 25 sales a month to cost less than that percentage. These are simplified numbers that ignore processor fees, but they show why the "right" platform changes as you grow.

There are also two other paths. A one-time-fee cart (ThriveCart is the usual example, historically around $495) trades a larger upfront payment for no monthly fee. And you can assemble it yourself with Stripe or PayPal payment links plus automation, which we cover in the delivery guide.

Which fits what you are building

  • Testing your first product, no budget. Gumroad or Payhip free. No monthly cost, so a flop costs nothing. You pay once you earn.
  • Selling through social media and a link in bio. Stan Store. It is designed around that flow and the setup is quick.
  • Running ads or a funnel with bumps and upsells. SamCart Pro, or an equivalent cart. Order bumps raise average order value without more ad spend.
  • Selling in many countries. Look hard at who handles VAT and sales tax. A merchant-of-record platform removes a headache.
  • Technical, and want lowest fees. Stripe and PayPal links on your own page, with automated delivery you set up once.

For what it is worth, we started on Stan Store and moved to SamCart. The reason was a specific feature need (offering PayPal alongside cards), not that one was wrong. Most sellers outgrow a tool the moment they need something it does not do. That is the signal to move, not before.

Stripe vs PayPal for digital products

If you assemble your own stack, or if your platform lets you choose, these are the two payment processors to understand. US standard online rates at the time of writing:

StripePayPal
Standard rate2.9% + $0.30 per domestic card payment3.49% + $0.49 for PayPal checkout. About 2.99% + $0.49 for standard cards.
WalletsApple Pay, Google Pay, and Link (saved details autofill) appear automatically when enabledPayPal balance, bank, Venmo (US)
InternationalExtra 1.5% on international cards, plus 1% for currency conversionExtra 1.5% international, and a currency conversion spread
Disputes$15 per dispute$15 standard dispute, $20 card chargeback
Best atSmooth card checkout, developer tools, subscriptionsBuyers who prefer a wallet they already trust

What this means in practice

  • Offering both usually beats offering one. Some buyers will not type a card number into a page they do not know but will click a PayPal button. Others want Apple Pay. Covering both removes a reason to abandon.
  • On a $27 sale Stripe takes about $1.08 and PayPal checkout about $1.43. Not large, but the fixed fee matters more as prices fall.
  • Digital goods and disputes. PayPal's seller protection generally does not cover digital goods, and funds can be held during a dispute. Keep clear delivery records either way.
  • Check your platform. Many carts connect to both. SamCart supports Stripe and PayPal, which is the reason some sellers pick it.

Fees change, so confirm on Stripe's and PayPal's pricing pages before you build a spreadsheet around them.

Test before you promote

Before any traffic, buy your own product (use a 100% coupon if your platform allows) and confirm four things:

  1. The payment goes through.
  2. The delivery email arrives and is not in spam.
  3. The download link works.
  4. The file opens on both desktop and phone.

Repeat the test whenever you change the product or delivery settings. Every broken link in this chain is a lost sale you never see.

    Questions people ask

    What price should my first digital product be?

    A common starting zone is $17 to $37 for a guide or small toolkit. It signals real value, and it leaves room to pay for advertising. Under $10 is hard to make profitable with paid traffic. Test and adjust.

    Do I need a platform like Stan Store or SamCart?

    No, but they save time. They bundle checkout, delivery, receipts and sometimes email. If you are comfortable assembling Stripe, PayPal and a little automation yourself, you can sell without a monthly platform fee. If you would rather not, a platform is a perfectly reasonable purchase.

    Is Stripe or PayPal better?

    They solve different problems. Stripe has the smoother card checkout and developer tools. PayPal has buyers who prefer paying with a wallet they already trust. Many sellers offer both.

    Do I have to collect sales tax?

    Rules vary by country and state. Some platforms act as the merchant of record and handle it. If you use Stripe or PayPal directly, you are responsible. Check your local rules or ask an accountant.
